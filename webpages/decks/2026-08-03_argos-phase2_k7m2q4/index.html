The principle · Evidence · on request

Write once, read everywhere

A fact is entered one time, by the person closest to it, and it appears everywhere it is needed. Three real examples from your own operation.

"This vendor is excluded, no response"

Today

The PM writes it in her tracker, then opens the supply chain tracker and copies it across one by one. If she does not, supply chain never knows and the next project contacts the person again.

After

One field. It appears on the Supply Chain Board, on the vendor record with its reason, in the funnel counts, and in the team memory next year.

"The agreed buy rate is 20 USD"

Today

Negotiated by supply chain, typed into the master tracker, copied by the PM into her tracker, typed again into XTRF at PO time. Three entries, three chances to diverge.

After

Entered once. Margin computes itself, rolls up to the project, and lands on your dashboard against target.

"Test result: borderline"

Today

Quality writes it in their tracker, then re-enters it in the PM's tracker in order to talk to them. Supply chain reads it somewhere else again.

After

One record. It updates the vendor history, the funnel, the performance flag and the Quality Board, in the same second.

One owner, one home, and everyone else gets a view, never a copy. That is the difference between ten spreadsheets and one system, and it is why the trackers can actually die.

Throughput & Delivery Forecast

Asked for by name on 4 August · inside the fee

"Forecasting throughput and delivery on a daily and weekly basis... that'll get up to a higher aggregate level of how we're tracking against milestones."

Mitchell Ison · 4 August 2026

What it reads

  • Every batch: volume committed, unit, and the date it is due
  • Volume processed to date, read from Myriad through the bridge
  • The staffing lines: who is on it, and at what daily capacity
  • Working days left before the milestone

What it answers, every morning

  • Units per day and per week, by batch and by project, actual against required
  • The delivery date the current run rate actually produces, next to the one you committed
  • The aggregate: how the BU is tracking against its milestones this week
  • Volume times unit price, so the same view reads in revenue, not only in units

What it needs to be honest

  • Volume recorded per batch, per day. Nothing does that today, which is why no forecast exists today. The layer records it from go-live.
  • Unit price on the project record and the milestone date on the batch, both entered at intake
  • History. The first weeks after go-live are a baseline, not a prediction, and we will say so rather than show you a confident curve built on two data points.

It lives as a tab on the GM Command Center and on the Program Cockpit, it is built in Sprint 3, and it is inside the fee. It is the one screen that turns the layer from a record of what happened into a projection of what lands.

The objection we would raise ourselves

Asana did not stick here. This is built the other way round.

Why it failed, in three lines

  • It was added beside the trackers, not instead of them. Nothing was retired, so the spreadsheets stayed the real system and Asana became a second place to update.
  • It held tasks, not the work. No vendor, no batch, no rate, no quality check, so the state of a project could never live there.
  • Nobody's morning changed. Opening it was extra effort with no view they could not get elsewhere, and extra effort always loses.

The structural difference, in three lines

  • It replaces the four trackers. At go-live they are frozen read-only. There is no second place to update, because there is no first place left.
  • It holds the nouns and the verb. Projects, batches, vendors, staffing, rates, quality checks. The state of the work has a home, so the spreadsheet has no job.
  • It is piloted on 2 to 3 live projects from Sprint 1, with one named champion per function trained during the build, not demonstrated at the end.

The adoption guarantee, with a number attached

Day 30

Measured together, 30 days after go-live. Not a feeling, not a survey.

The criterion

The four trackers are frozen read-only, and every project opened after go-live was created in Notion. Both true, or the criterion is missed.

The adoption window extends from 30 to 90 days at no extra cost, with a weekly working session on the blockers, until both are true. Matching a brief is not adoption. Asana matched its brief too.
